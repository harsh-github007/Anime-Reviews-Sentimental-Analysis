How discussion sentiment works

AniList threads linked to each anime are sampled newest thread first. Replies and nested replies are deduplicated by comment ID, up to 5,000 per title. VADER predicts positive, neutral and negative language using compound cut-offs of ±0.05. Comments can discuss characters, other viewers or unrelated subjects; these percentages do not measure recommendation rates or validated accuracy. Smaller totals mean fewer comments were available or collection is incomplete.

Read the evaluation limits

Models are evaluated with folds separated by anime, so the trained model does not see reviews from the held-out show. Tuned cut-offs use training folds only. This small snapshot is unevenly distributed across shows; differences between methods should be read alongside confidence intervals, not treated as a universal ranking.

03 / THE CONTEXT

Read the review.
Respect the verdict.

Re-run in Colab ↗
  1. 01

    Collect recent reviews

    The pipeline samples MyAnimeList’s top airing titles and keeps the reviewer’s Recommended, Mixed Feelings, or Not Recommended verdict. Preliminary reviews may be included.

  2. 02

    Compare words with conclusions

    TextBlob and VADER score the language. A TF-IDF model learns from verdicts. A negative review may still praise animation, music, or earlier episodes.

  3. 03

    Test on unseen shows

    Evaluation splits by anime and resamples whole shows for uncertainty. This frontend reads saved aggregate outputs; it does not run inference or collect user text.
